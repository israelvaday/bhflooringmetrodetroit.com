How to Hire a Flooring Contractor in Michigan: Written Scope Checklist

Your Essential Checklist for Hiring a Flooring Contractor in Michigan

Embarking on a flooring project, whether for your home in Ann Arbor or your business in downtown Detroit, is a significant investment. To ensure a smooth process, a high-quality outcome, and peace of mind, hiring the right flooring contractor is paramount. In Michigan, with its specific licensing requirements and diverse housing stock, due diligence is key. BH Flooring Metro Detroit, a licensed contractor serving Wayne, Oakland, and Macomb counties, provides this checklist to help you make an informed decision and ensure you get a comprehensive written scope of work.

1. Verify Licensing and Insurance

This is non-negotiable. In Michigan, reputable contractors are licensed and insured. Do not proceed with anyone who cannot provide proof.

  • Contractor's License: Ask for their Michigan Residential Builder or Maintenance & Alteration Contractor license number. Verify it with the State of Michigan's Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA).
  • Insurance: Request proof of liability insurance (to cover damages to your property) and workers' compensation insurance (to protect you from liability if a worker is injured on your property).

2. Request a Detailed Written Scope of Work and Estimate

A verbal quote is insufficient. A professional contractor will provide a clear, itemized written estimate that details every aspect of the project. This is your blueprint for the job.

Your Written Scope Checklist Should Include:

  • Specific Areas: Clearly define which rooms or areas will receive new flooring.
  • Material Specification: Exact type, brand, color, and quantity of flooring material (e.g., "1,000 sq ft of Mohawk RevWood Premier LVP, 'Coastal Driftwood' color").
  • Subfloor Preparation: Detail any necessary subfloor work, such as leveling, patching, moisture mitigation, or removal of old flooring and adhesives. This is crucial for a lasting installation, especially in Michigan's varying climates.
  • Removal and Disposal: Clearly state who is responsible for removing old flooring and how it will be disposed of.
  • Furniture Moving: Who will move furniture? Is there an additional charge?
  • Installation Method: Specify the installation method (e.g., nail-down hardwood, glue-down LVP, floating floor).
  • Trim Work: Will existing baseboards be removed and reinstalled? Will quarter-round or shoe molding be installed?
  • Transitions: Detail how new flooring will transition to existing flooring in adjacent rooms.
  • Timeline: A realistic start and completion date, including any potential milestones.
  • Payment Schedule: Clearly outlined payment terms (e.g., deposit, progress payments, final payment upon completion).
  • Warranty Information: Details on both the product warranty and the contractor's labor warranty.
  • Cleanup: What level of cleanup is included after the project is finished?

3. Check References and Reviews

A contractor's past work and client satisfaction speak volumes.

  • References: Ask for at least three recent references from projects similar to yours. Call them and ask about communication, adherence to schedule, quality of work, and how issues were handled.
  • Online Reviews: Check platforms like Google, Yelp, and the Better Business Bureau. Look for consistent positive feedback and how the contractor responds to negative reviews.

4. Ask About Experience and Specializations

Different flooring types require different expertise. Ensure your contractor has experience with your chosen material.

  • Years in Business: Longevity often indicates stability and experience.
  • Specific Material Expertise: If you're installing complex tile patterns or intricate hardwood designs, ensure they have proven experience in those areas.
  • Michigan Climate Knowledge: Do they understand how Michigan's humidity and temperature fluctuations affect certain flooring types, particularly hardwood and engineered wood?

5. Communication and Professionalism

Pay attention to how the contractor communicates from the first interaction.

  • Responsiveness: Do they respond to your inquiries promptly and thoroughly?
  • Clarity: Do they explain the process clearly and answer your questions patiently?
  • Professional Demeanor: Do they arrive on time for appointments and present themselves professionally?

6. Never Pay the Full Amount Upfront

Be wary of contractors who demand full payment before any work begins. A reasonable payment schedule typically includes a deposit, progress payments, and a final payment upon satisfactory completion of the project.
