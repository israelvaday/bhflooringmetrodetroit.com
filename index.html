Buyer's guide

How to hire a flooring contractor in Metro Detroit

A good flooring bid is specific. A bad one is a single number with no material spec, no subfloor prep, and no start date. Use this checklist before you sign.

1. Verify license and insurance. Ask for proof of general liability and workers comp. For commercial jobs, request a certificate naming your building or GC as additional insured.

2. Define materials in writing. Product name, thickness, wear layer (LVP), or hardwood grade should be on the quote — not just "install flooring."

3. Subfloor prep scope. Leveling, moisture mitigation, and demo of old flooring should be listed separately so you are not surprised mid-job.

4. Transitions and trim. Door thresholds, stair noses, and baseboard re-install should be spelled out. Know who moves furniture back in.

5. Schedule and access. Occupied homes need daily cleanup. Commercial TI needs phased work and after-hours if the suite stays open.

6. Change orders. Extra demo, subfloor repair, or pattern changes should be written and signed — not verbal add-ons at invoice time.

BH Flooring Metro Detroit provides written estimates, serves Wayne, Oakland, and Macomb counties, and answers questions at (313) 236-4558. Compare two bids using the same material and prep scope — then decide on crew quality and schedule, not just the lowest number.

Glossary

Flooring terms, explained in plain language

Whether you are comparing bids in Warren or planning a basement remodel in Livonia, these are the terms BH Flooring Metro Detroit uses on every estimate — so you know exactly what you are paying for.

LVP / WPC — luxury vinyl plank and wood-plastic composite — waterproof click-lock flooring popular in Michigan basements and kitchens.

Engineered hardwood — real wood veneer over plywood — more stable than solid wood on slabs and over radiant heat.

Subfloor prep — leveling, patching, and moisture testing before new floor goes down. Skipping prep causes squeaks and plank separation.

Underlayment — pad or membrane under floating floors for sound, moisture, and minor slab irregularities.

Transition strip — metal or wood profile where one floor type meets another at doorways, stairs, or tile edges.

Sand & refinish — stripping old finish, sanding bare wood, staining, and applying polyurethane — often cheaper than full replacement for solid hardwood.

Moisture mitigation — epoxy or vapor-barrier systems on concrete slabs before adhesive or floating installs in basements.

Punch list — final walkthrough items — gaps at baseboards, loose transitions, touch-up planks — before move-in.
